\section{Common Experimental Problems and Limits of Applicability}
\label{sec:failure-boundaries}

The range over which a method applies can be judged only when failed or anomalous results are recorded together with the composition, the experimental conditions, and the product characterization. This section covers phase competition, contamination and volatilization, atmosphere and polymorphism, missing information, and environmental, health, and safety (EHS) constraints. It is confined to published conditions and does not turn these boundaries into new experimental protocols.

\paragraph{Phase regions and phase competition.}
The systematic comparison of flux growth across the Ba--Y silicate family shows that phase-formation outcomes must be read against the composition and route variables; no single heat-treatment condition can be lifted out as a general one~\cite{wierzbickawieczorek2017high}. Later work, through re-examination of the non-stoichiometric structure, ceramic composition series, and several diffraction methods, revealed modulated structures, secondary phases, and competing phases or glass-forming regions~\cite{yamane2024synthesis,yamane2024microstructure,gulay2024navigation}. These results delimit only the composition ranges each study actually examined; they cannot show that no other phases exist in the regions left uncovered. Mechanochemical studies indicate only that pre-activation may influence subsequent phase formation, and a particular negative result cannot be read as an intrinsic phase boundary of the target phase~\cite{tzvetkov2001effects}. The products, characterization, and synthesis conditions in Tables~\ref{tab:synthesis-conditions} and~\ref{tab:synthesis-conditions-b} must therefore come from the same publication, and impurity phases or glass cannot be interpreted apart from the composition and thermal history.

\paragraph{Contamination, volatilization, and flux boundaries.}
The Zn-free Ba--Y ceramic study traced the secondary phases and the agate-derived \(\mathrm{SiO_2}\) contamination to specific processing steps: an apparent departure from the nominal ratio should first prompt a check of the starting materials and the processing, and cannot automatically be taken as evidence of genuine solid solution~\cite{yamane2024microstructure}. The Czochralski study of the Y--Si--O system offers reference points for the melt, crucible, atmosphere, and pulling conditions, but the available material is not enough to judge whether the target phase loses Zn or Si by volatilization~\cite{shoudu1999czochralski}. Lead- and fluorine-containing fluxes were once used to grow Zn--Si--O crystals, but those early conditions serve only for comparing slow cooling, separation, and the role of the flux; they are no basis for choosing materials for the target phase~\cite{giess1982zn2sio4}. Pickup from the grinding media, mass loss in open systems, and flux residues or flux-driven phase selection should therefore be recorded separately; where the literature gives no pre- and post-firing compositions or residue analysis, this review assigns no specific failure mechanism.

\paragraph{Atmosphere and polymorphism.}
The low-temperature monoclinic and high-temperature orthorhombic phases of BaZn$_2$Si$_2$O$_7$ were distinguished by X-ray and neutron diffraction, so the in situ high-temperature phase and the cooled product are not interchangeable~\cite{lin1999phase}. High-temperature diffraction further shows that Zn\slash Co substitution and the composition of the Ba\slash Sr--Zn--Si\slash Ge solid solutions are coupled to phase stability or polymorph boundaries; if composition, temperature, or the time point of observation is missing, the stability range cannot be defined~\cite{kerstan2013bazn2si2o7,thieme2016negative}. The reduction-resistance report on a related Ba--Zn--Si ceramic shows only that that compound was tested for atmosphere tolerance under the published conditions; it cannot be extrapolated to the reduction stability of the target phase~\cite{zou2019anti}. Other comparisons of thermal expansion and high-temperature diffraction likewise serve only as references for thermally induced change~\cite{kerstan2012thermal}.

\paragraph{Missing information and EHS.}
Some publications give only the synthesis method or the structural conclusion without the full set of ratios, temperature--time program, atmosphere, crucible, and cooling~\cite{ababaikeri2024ba5y12zn,ababaikeri2024ba3zn4si4o15,kaiser2002crystal,cai2024optimized,zhao2026crystal}. This means only that this review has not obtained the information; it does not mean that the original paper failed to report it, nor that the step was absent from the experiment. Table~\ref{tab:synthesis-conditions} marks such entries with ``---'' and preserves that boundary. The less complete the record, the fewer specific conditions can be drawn from it. EHS boundaries are likewise described only as published: Ba-containing powders, species volatile at high temperature, the Pb\slash F flux conditions of the early literature, crucible compatibility, and waste streams each require separate review, and the Pb\slash F routes in particular must not be promoted from early examples into recommended protocols~\cite{giess1982zn2sio4}. Any reuse must pass independent EHS, container-compatibility, and waste-segregation review; this review supplies no operating parameters and makes no presumption of compliance about ventilation, protective measures, equipment, or waste classification that the sources do not report.

These four kinds of boundary cannot be collapsed into a single unverified failure mechanism. Phase competition holds only within the published composition and thermal-history ranges; contamination or mass loss needs support from analysis of the media, of the pre- and post-firing compositions, or of residues; judgments about atmosphere and polymorphism must keep both the observation temperature and the state after cooling; and missing information marks only the limit of present knowledge. Accordingly, where the literature reports only ``accompanied by'' or gives only structural results, the text says ``accompanied by'' or ``consistent with'', never ``caused''. This keeps both negative results and records of success while avoiding any conflation of unreported items, process anomalies, and genuine phase regions.
